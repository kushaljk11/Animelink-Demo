Rabies Track Nepal can include AI as a **supporting component**, not as the main system.

### 1. AI Photo-Based Breed Finder

Users can upload a pet photo, and the system will analyze the image to suggest the most likely breed or breed group.

Flow:

`Pet Photo Upload → AI Image Analysis → Breed Suggestion → User Confirms/Edits → Saved to Pet Profile`

The AI result should be treated as a **suggestion**, because mixed breeds, image quality, lighting, and similar-looking breeds can reduce accuracy.

### 2. AI Chatbot

The system can include an AI chatbot to help users with:

- Navigating the platform
- Understanding pet vaccination schedules
- Explaining how QR pet identification works
- Guiding users through lost-pet reporting
- Explaining how to submit bite/suspected-rabies reports
- Helping users find relevant vets or shelters
- Providing general pet-care information

### 3. Safety Limitation

The chatbot should **not diagnose rabies or other medical conditions** and should not replace veterinarians or medical professionals.

For high-risk queries, such as a user saying they were bitten by an animal, the chatbot should guide them toward appropriate professional help rather than attempting a diagnosis.

### 4. AI Data Handling

Only the information necessary for the AI feature should be sent to the AI service.

Sensitive user information should not be unnecessarily exposed.

### 5. AI Evaluation

For the FYP, the AI features can also be evaluated.

For example:

**Breed Finder**

- Prediction accuracy
- Confidence score
- User confirmation rate

**Chatbot**

- Relevance of responses
- Helpfulness
- Response time
- Ability to avoid unsafe medical conclusions

### Overall AI Flow

`User Input → AI Service → Suggested Response/Prediction → User Review → System Action`

The AI should **assist users**, while important actions such as vaccination verification, medical records, and official incident handling remain under authorized human control.
